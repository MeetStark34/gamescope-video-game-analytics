# GameScope Advanced Analysis and Methodology

Generated by the analytics modules in `python/` from the cleaned CSVs. The primary assignment analysis uses **`vgsales.csv` only**. Advanced critic-score, developer, release-date, publisher, and console outputs use **`vgchartz-2024.csv` only**. The files have different coverage, fields, and regional definitions; no rows are joined or pooled.

Run `python -m python.pipeline` to refresh the cleaned inputs, then `python -m python.analytics_pipeline` to regenerate JSON/CSV outputs under `data/processed/analytics/`. The manifest identifies each source and row count. Missing inputs remain unavailable; the pipeline does not fill in sales, ratings, dates, publishers, or developers.

## Market Power Index (`vgsales.csv`)

The assignment does not prescribe a composite equation or component weights. The project uses a transparent **equal-weight arithmetic mean**, because the five dimensions are measured on a common 0-to-1 scale and no evidence in the specification supports privileging one dimension. This is a neutral, reproducible baseline, not an empirically optimized or objectively canonical weighting. The component values remain in every publisher record so alternatives can be inspected.

For publisher \(p\), genre \(g\), and source row \(i\):

1. **Publisher market share within genre**

   \[
   S_{p,g}=\frac{\sum_i GlobalSales_i\,1(Publisher_i=p,Genre_i=g)}
   {\sum_i GlobalSales_i\,1(Genre_i=g)}.
   \]

   Only numeric global sales and nonblank genre labels enter the denominator; rows without a publisher still contribute to total genre sales. A publisher's genre sales are attributed from rows with that publisher label. Values are exported as `publisher_genre_market_shares` with the numerator, denominator, and share.

2. **Genre leadership**

   \[
   L_{p,g}=1(S_{p,g}>0.30),\qquad
   LC_p=\frac{\sum_g L_{p,g}}{G},
   \]

   where the threshold is strictly greater than 30%, as required, and \(G\) is the number of observed genre labels. Equality at 30% is not leadership. `genre_leader_count` and `genre_leaders` preserve the unnormalized result; `genre_leadership_coverage` is the normalized component.

3. **Genre Diversity Score**

   \[
   D_p=\frac{\text{distinct observed genres represented by }p}{G}.
   \]

   The distinct genre count is also exported. This normalized share is comparable across data versions with different numbers of genres.

4. **Regional Consistency Score**

   For each publisher, sum `NA_Sales`, `EU_Sales`, `JP_Sales`, and `Other_Sales` across its records where **all four values are present**. Divide each region's sum by the publisher's four-region total to get \(q_{p,r}\). Let \(\sigma_p\) be the population standard deviation of these four proportions. Then:

   \[
   RC_p=\max\left(0,\min\left(1,1-\frac{\sigma_p}{\sqrt{3}/4}\right)\right).
   \]

   The maximum possible population standard deviation of four probabilities summing to one is \(\sqrt{3}/4\), attained when all share is in one region; equal regional shares have standard deviation zero. The normalization therefore maps most even sales distributions to 1 and the most concentrated distribution to 0. A publisher with no complete-case regional sales total has an unavailable score. Incomplete rows are counted but excluded from this component only.

5. **Hit Rate**

   For each genre, compute the median `Global_Sales` over all rows with valid genre and sales. For a publisher, the denominator is its records with valid genre and sales. A record is a hit only when its sales are **strictly greater** than its genre median:

   \[
   H_p=\frac{\#\{i:Publisher_i=p, GlobalSales_i>MedianSales_{Genre_i}\}}
   {\#\{i:Publisher_i=p, Genre_i\ valid, GlobalSales_i\ valid\}}.
   \]

   The unit is a source record (game/platform record), not a deduplicated title. Ties at the median are not hits.

6. **Sales Share and Market Power Index**

   \[
   MS_p=\frac{\sum_i GlobalSales_i\,1(Publisher_i=p)}
   {\sum_i GlobalSales_i\,1(Publisher_i\ is\ nonblank)};
   \quad
   MPI_p=\frac{100}{|A_p|}\sum_{c\in A_p}c_p,
   \]

   where \(A_p\) is the set of available components from \(\{MS_p,LC_p,D_p,RC_p,H_p\}\). The arithmetic mean gives each normalized dimension 20% when all are available. If a component cannot be computed, the remaining available components receive equal shares and `available_component_count` makes that coverage visible. Ranks sort descending by score; alphabetical publisher text breaks score ties. Unscored publishers receive no rank. The output formula version is `mpi_equal_weight_arithmetic_v2`.

7. **Publisher archetypes**

   “Diversified” means at least half of all observed genres. “Leader” means at least one genre above the strict 30% threshold. Two or more leaders plus broad diversity yields **Diversified Genre Leader**; a publisher with a leader and at most two represented genres is **Genre Specialist**; broad diversity without that first rule is **Diversified Publisher**; other publishers with any leader are **Genre Leader**; otherwise the category is **No 30% Genre Leadership Flag**. These rules are transparent display groupings, not inferred latent classes.

The MPI is compensatory: a higher component may offset a lower one. It summarizes this selected set of dimensions, not publisher quality, profitability, market power in a legal sense, or causal influence. The outputs support component-level review; no empirical weight calibration or alternate-weight rank-stability study is claimed.

## Regional Preference Divergence (`vgsales.csv`)

1. **Regional Divergence Score per game record:** use North America, Europe, Japan, and Other. The baseline \(b_r\) is region \(r\)'s share of total regional sales over records complete in all four regional columns. For each complete record with positive regional total, normalize its regional sales to \(p_{i,r}\), then calculate total variation distance:

   \[
   RDS_i=\frac{1}{2}\sum_r|p_{i,r}-b_r|.
   \]

   This is bounded by 0 and 1: zero means the game's regional mix matches the source baseline; one means no regional share overlaps. Rows with a missing regional value or zero regional total are ineligible for this score; they remain in all other applicable analyses.

2. **Genre-region affinity:** for genre \(g\), let \(s_{g,r}\) be its regional sales and \(S_g=\sum_r s_{g,r}\). Let \(B_r\) be all reported sales in region \(r\), and \(B=\sum_rB_r\). Then:

   \[
   A_{g,r}=\frac{s_{g,r}/S_g}{B_r/B}.
   \]

   The neutral index is exactly 1. Values above 1 are labeled `over_indexed`; values below 1 are `under_indexed`. This continuous comparison avoids an arbitrary distance-from-1 cutoff. A zero denominator produces an unavailable index.

3. **Temporal changes:** yearly regional genre shares are computed from sales by release year and region. `regional_preference_changes` reports each genre's share and its absolute difference from the previous **observed** year for that region; missing calendar years are not interpolated. The favorite genre is the highest-sales genre, with alphabetical tie-breaking.

4. **Preference stability:** within each region, count adjacent observed-year pairs that have the same favorite genre and divide by all adjacent observed-year pairs. This is a 0-to-1 descriptive repetition rate; pairs can span gaps in calendar years. It is not a forecast or an assertion of cultural continuity.

5. **Cultural Specificity Score and outlier screen:** for each eligible game, let \(p_{max}\) be its largest regional share and \(p_{other,max}\) the largest share among remaining regions:

   \[
   CSS_i=100(p_{max}-p_{other,max}).
   \]

   Scores range from 0 to 100. The specified screen flags a record only when one region exceeds 50% and **each** other region is below 10%. `regional_divergence_by_game` includes the score and Boolean screen result for every eligible record; `cultural_outliers` contains flagged records. Missing-region and zero-total behavior follows the complete-case rule above. These labels describe sales concentration, not a proven cultural cause.

## Supplemental Advanced Analysis (`vgchartz-2024.csv` only)

### Critic score versus total sales

Valid pairs require critic score in [0, 10] and nonnegative `total_sales`. Missing, unparseable, out-of-range, and negative values are counted separately; the pair count is always reported. Pearson \(r\) measures linear association on raw sales. Spearman \(\rho\) is Pearson correlation of average ranks (ties receive their mean rank). A second Pearson coefficient uses `ln(1 + total_sales)` to show sensitivity to the long-tailed sales scale. Equal-width one-point rating bands report record count, mean sales, and median sales. These are descriptive associations. The observed rows are a selected subset with both values; neither correlations nor band summaries establish that critic ratings cause sales.

### Developers and publishers

Publisher, developer, and publisher/developer pair summaries use literal cleaned labels. Blank labels are unavailable; nonblank sentinels such as `Unknown` remain literal categories. Valid nonnegative sales and in-range ratings contribute to their respective measures. Counts expose each measure's coverage. Names are not entity-resolved, subsidiaries are not consolidated, and records are not deduplicated.

### Console evolution, release dates, and recent trends

- Console-year tables count records with a valid ISO release date and aggregate valid nonnegative sales; lifecycle summaries report first/last observed year, active observed years, and peak observed annual sales.
- Calendar summaries report releases by year, quarter, month, and weekday. ISO parsing confirms a valid `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date, not the source's actual date precision. The audit found many Jan 1 values, so these remain included but are surfaced as possible year-only placeholders.
- “Recent” means the ten-year window ending at the maximum valid release year in the supplemental source; it does not move with the machine clock. Annual changes compare adjacent observed years and are unavailable when the prior denominator is zero. The 2015–2024 window is what the current cleaned source produces.

Console is not silently treated as equivalent to primary-source Platform. Supplemental PAL sales are not renamed to Europe. None of these outputs are used to backfill or alter the primary assignment analyses.

## Findings from the current run

- The primary dataset has 16,598 rows. The current MPI ranking begins with Nintendo (59.44), Electronic Arts (49.64), and Sony Computer Entertainment (48.99); the score reflects only the documented five-component formula and current source coverage.
- Regional divergence is computable for 16,597 primary records. The >50% / <10% regional screen flags 6,524 eligible records. This is a rule-based concentration screen and should not be read as proof of cultural preference.
- The supplemental dataset has 64,016 rows. Critic score is present on 6,541 rows, total sales on 18,520, but only 4,043 rows (6.32% of all rows) contain both in-range score and nonnegative sales. On that paired sample, Pearson raw-sales correlation is 0.284, Spearman rank correlation is 0.344, and Pearson correlation with log1p sales is 0.348. These positive associations are modest and conditional on the small complete-pair subset.
- Score-band medians generally increase in the populated bands, while means are affected by the long sales tail; the 9–10 band contains 276 pairs. This is consistent with stronger sales appearing alongside higher ratings in this subset, not a causal claim.
- The supplemental source has 55,564 valid ISO release dates from 1971 to 2024; 4,871 are January 1 dates flagged as potentially imprecise. The trailing observed ten-year summary is 2015–2024.
