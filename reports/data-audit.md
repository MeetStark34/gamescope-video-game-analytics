## Missing values: strategies to evaluate, no policy selected

This audit distinguishes empty cells, candidate null-like spellings (`N/A`, `NA`, `null`, `None`, `NaN`, `<NA>`), and placeholder categories such as `Unknown`, `-`, and `TBD`. Whether an individual token denotes missingness or a literal category remains a data-policy decision. The report does not choose a strategy or alter values. Before cleaning, document a field-specific decision and its rationale.

Possible options include:

1. **Retain as missing and report coverage.** Suitable when imputation would invent sales, score, year, or publisher information; use metric-specific complete-case denominators.
2. **Impute only with a defensible domain method.** Document method, training/cohort boundaries, and an imputation flag. Avoid imputing sales or critic scores without strong justification.
3. **Exclude rows for a particular metric only.** Keep the row in other analyses and publish each metric’s eligible row count; do not globally drop rows because one field is absent.
4. **Keep unknown categories distinct.** Treat `Unknown`, `TBD`, and blank as separate raw states until provenance establishes that they mean the same thing. A display label such as “Unspecified” can be produced without overwriting source data.
5. **Resolve or drop duplicate candidates only after review.** Compare all fields and source identity; document removed rows and retain an audit trail.

No missing-value, duplicate, spelling, or outlier policy has been selected by this audit.
